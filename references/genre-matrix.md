# Persian Genre and Register Matrix

Use this reference when a text's communicative setting materially affects what counts as natural Persian. "Human" is not one register.

| Genre | Natural tendencies | Legitimate explicitness/repetition | Common Humanizer failure | Protect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Casual conversation | spoken syntax, contractions, ellipsis, simple chaining | shared context can remain implicit | formalizing into written standard Persian | `رو`, `یه`, `می‌خوام`, dialect, relationship tone |
| Semi-formal communication | modern direct Persian, light spoken cadence | concise context and politeness | corporate or bureaucratic rewrite | brevity, relationship tone |
| Professional/business | direct, courteous, stable business terms | decisions, status, ownership, deadlines explicit | bureaucratizing or over-casualizing | commitments, responsibility, dates |
| Blog/article | accessible prose, writer-specific rhythm | examples and rhetorical questions can be natural | generic intro, headings everywhere, forced conclusion | author voice and argument |
| Journalism/news | event/actor/time/consequence orientation | repeated names may support attribution | essay framing or marketing tone | attribution, chronology, uncertainty |
| Academic/research | technical vocabulary, hedging, complex syntax when needed | nominalization, citations, explicit reasoning | casualizing, deleting hedges, inflating significance | methodology, modality, citations, terminology |
| Technical documentation | stable terms, direct imperatives/declaratives | exact term repetition is desirable | synonym rotation, translating identifiers | code, commands, API names, sequence, warnings |
| Product documentation | user-centered and precise | UI labels and capability boundaries repeated consistently | inserting marketing claims | labels, supported capabilities, conditions |
| Marketing/advertising | brand voice, compressed syntax, persuasion | slogan rhythm and intentional repetition can be valid | neutralizing persuasion or inventing proof | claims, slogans, CTA, brand terms |
| Social media | platform-specific brevity, mixed spoken/written style | fragments and code-switching can be natural | turning post into mini-essay | voice, contractions, hashtags when purposeful |
| Customer support | acknowledgement, status, next action | functional politeness | chatbot ceremony or excessive terseness | policy, ticket IDs, commitments, current status |
| Corporate reports | controlled formality, metrics, stable business labels | repetition may support governance/traceability | over-simplifying institutional language | defined terms, metrics, attribution |
| Formal letters | conventional salutation/closing, precise request/status | formulaic etiquette may be expected | treating all ceremony as slop | institutional politeness and requested action |
| Government/administrative | standardized terminology, passive/nominal style often conventional | procedure and authority explicit | replacing mandated language because it sounds bureaucratic | procedural/legal force and fixed templates |
| Opinion/editorial | distinct stance, rhetoric, evaluative vocabulary | repetition, contrast, asymmetry can be intentional | enforcing artificial neutrality | position, irony, intensity, genuine caveats |

## Mixed-register texts

Many real documents contain more than one register.

Examples:

- casual blog narration containing exact API documentation;
- social post quoting a formal announcement;
- corporate email containing a legal clause;
- support response containing a policy excerpt.

Do not assign one style to the entire artifact. Protect embedded sections according to their local function.

## When register signals conflict

Prefer, in order:

1. explicit user instruction;
2. channel and communicative purpose;
3. dominant source register;
4. local embedded genre;
5. existing writer voice.

For high-stakes text where a rewrite could alter legal, academic, or technical function, choose `FLAG` instead of guessing.
